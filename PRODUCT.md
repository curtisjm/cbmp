# Product

## Register

product

## Users

CBMP is used by people who run, support, and participate in collegiate ballroom competitions: Competition Hosts, Organization Admins, Scrutineers, Judges, Deck Captains, Competitors, and Platform Admins. They use the product during planning, setup, registration-facing workflows, and day-of operations where competition state needs to be understood quickly and changed carefully.

Users need reliable operational surfaces for Competition setup, public Competition discovery, Entries, Competition Lifecycle state, schedules, official assignments, scoring-adjacent workflows, and role-specific decisions. They may be working from laptops or tablets in imperfect lighting, near time-sensitive floor operations, or between repeated administrative tasks.

## Product Purpose

CBMP exists to make ballroom Competition management clearer, faster, and safer for college ballroom organizations and competition staff. It should give each role the information and actions they need without implying authority they do not have.

Success means users can understand what state a Competition is in, what needs attention, and which actions are available to them without scanning through generic admin noise. Public routes should frame the product clearly, while authenticated and operational routes should prioritize repeatable work, domain clarity, and trustworthy state changes.

## Brand Personality

Composed, precise, operational.

The product should feel calm and competent under pressure, with enough visual character to avoid a generic admin template. It should be modern and dense, but not cold, decorative, or theatrical. Creativity belongs in the treatment of operational information surfaces, such as lifecycle state, schedules, tables, empty states, and route structure, while core controls remain familiar.

## Anti-references

Operational app surfaces should not feel like marketing pages, decorative ballroom event sites, analytics theater, or generic SaaS admin templates. CBMP can eventually include a public marketing landing page, clean modern decorative moments, and real analytics pages; those surfaces should still feel connected to the composed product language and real domain data. Avoid oversized hero sections on operational routes, ornamental card grids, ballroom cliches, fake actor dashboards before product decisions define them, and placeholder content that implies unsettled domain decisions.

When a design pass explicitly asks to copy a provided visual reference, bounded reference rows may be used on that surface to match the composition. Treat those rows as UI reference material only; they do not establish live data behavior, seeded development data, or domain authorization decisions.

Do not use unfamiliar controls for standard actions. Buttons, inputs, menus, tabs, dialogs, and badges should remain predictable, accessible, and shadcn-compatible, with custom visual language applied deliberately rather than through novelty.

## Design Principles

- Keep core controls familiar and make competition operations distinctive.
- Prioritize clarity under time pressure.
- Treat statuses, schedules, tables, lifecycle state, and empty states as first-class visual surfaces.
- Use restraint by default, with deliberate color, rounding, density, and motion only where they improve comprehension.
- Preserve domain language from the glossary, especially User Profile, Competition, Entry, Competition Lifecycle, Scrutineer, Judge, Deck Captain, and Organization.
- Avoid implying product behavior or role authority that has not been defined by PRDs or domain decisions.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Status, lifecycle, and action states must not rely on color alone. Motion should be sparse and functional, with reduced-motion support for every animated interaction. Text, controls, tables, badges, and empty states should remain readable in imperfect competition-day environments and on laptop or tablet screens.
